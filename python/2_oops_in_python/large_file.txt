The President of India is the highest constitutional authority,
 serving as the head of state, the first citizen of the nation, 
 and the supreme commander of the Indian Armed Forces.
  Governed primarily by Part V (Articles 52 to 78) of the 
  Indian Constitution, the office acts as the anchor of the 
  republic, representing national unity and constitutional 
  continuity. While the framework vests vast executive 
  authority in the office, India’s parliamentary democracy operates
   on a dual-executive principle, making the President the 
   nominal head of government, while real executive power 
   resides with the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers.
   The Indian political model is deeply influenced by the British
    Westminster system, yet it diverges fundamentally by functioning 
    as a republic rather than a constitutional monarchy. 
    Article 52 of the Constitution explicitly states that 
    "There shall be a President of India," establishing the
     permanence of the office. Under Article 53, the executive 
     power of the Union is formally vested in the President, 
     to be exercised either directly or through subordinate 
     officers in accordance with the Constitution.
